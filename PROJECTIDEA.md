My project will examine the economic ramifications of persistent litter accumulation in coastal and beach environments, investigating the extent to which environmental degradation translates into quantifiable financial losses for local communities and economies. Specifically, I aim to analyze the relationship between the prevalence of litter and key economic indicators, including tourism expenditure, local business revenue, municipal sanitation costs, property values, and visitor attendance, in order to determine whether deteriorating coastal conditions generate measurable reductions in economic activity. This issue is particularly significant because litter constitutes a negative environmental externality: although the immediate act of disposal may impose little apparent cost on the individual, its cumulative consequences redistribute substantial financial burdens onto taxpayers, businesses, governments, and communities while simultaneously diminishing the recreational and commercial value of coastal areas. My analysis will utilize quantitative datasets encompassing litter density and composition, annual beach-cleanup expenditures, tourist visitation rates, tourism-generated revenue, local business performance, and other socioeconomic indicators to identify correlations and potentially estimate the broader economic cost attributable to coastal litter pollution.
